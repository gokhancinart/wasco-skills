# WASCO WEAR --- Brand & Creative Operations Skill

## 1. Purpose

Use this document as the standing reference for WASCO WEAR tasks. Apply
confirmed decisions consistently, avoid asking the user to repeat
settled information, and distinguish confirmed facts from assumptions or
open decisions. If a new instruction conflicts with this guide, follow
the user's latest explicit instruction for that task and flag any
lasting change that should be added to this guide.

## 2. Confirmed brand context

-   **Brand name:** WASCO WEAR
-   **Primary market:** Türkiye first.
-   **Audience:** People aged approximately 18--35 interested in
    premium-looking streetwear and character-inspired design.
-   **Positioning:** Independent streetwear brand; not merely a generic
    anime merchandise page.
-   **Core product categories:** Hoodies, T-shirts, sweatshirts, and
    caps.
-   **Design philosophy:** A simple, restrained brand identity with
    freedom to vary the character-inspired artwork. Collections may
    differ in theme, but the brand should remain recognizable.
-   **Fit:** May vary by product; do not assume every item is oversized.
-   **Embroidery:** Size, placement, density, and detail level vary
    according to the design and garment.
-   **Price direction:** Mid-market. A preliminary hoodie selling-price
    range of **₺1,500--₺2,000** has been considered, but it is not a
    final price list. Prices for other products are undecided.
-   **Production model:** Source ready-made garments and have embroidery
    applied; produce to order and/or in small batches.
-   **Initial launch:** Start with a small collection of a few strong
    designs rather than a large assortment.
-   **Sales channels:** Instagram DMs and an online store.
-   **Creative production:** Use real embroidery samples as key visual
    references. Use Higgsfield for AI-assisted product imagery and
    realistic videos that emphasize the garment and embroidery.
-   **Character direction:** Focus on original designs inspired by
    anime/cartoon culture rather than assuming that direct commercial
    use of protected characters is permitted.

## 3. Brand principles

1.  **Embroidery is the hero.** Show the stitch texture, thread
    direction, edge quality, relief, and how the embroidery sits on the
    fabric.
2.  **Wearable before novelty.** Designs should feel like clothing
    people want to wear, not only fan collectibles.
3.  **Distinctive but coherent.** Different collections can have
    different moods, palettes, and motifs; keep a consistent restrained
    brand identity, product quality, and presentation standard.
4.  **Authenticity over AI spectacle.** AI should improve presentation,
    not misrepresent the actual garment or embroidery.
5.  **Small-batch discipline.** Validate designs and costs before
    expanding stock or the number of SKUs.
6.  **Truthful selling.** Never invent garment specifications, fabric
    weights, fit, production time, stock, shipping terms, or quality
    claims. Ask or mark as unconfirmed.
7.  **Commercially aware originality.** Avoid treating an existing
    character, logo, distinctive costume, or franchise artwork as
    automatically available for commercial use. If the concept relies on
    recognizable protected IP, flag the rights question and propose an
    original alternative or properly licensed route.

## 4. Visual identity rules

### Confirmed

-   Keep the WASCO WEAR logo simple.
-   Let character-inspired designs carry more of the visual expression.
-   Maintain a recognizable brand identity across varied collections.

### Not yet decided

-   Exact logo artwork and lockups.
-   Official color palette and typography.
-   Packaging, labels, woven tags, and garment branding.
-   Official slogan or tagline.
-   Exact art direction for each collection.

Do not invent these as already approved. Offer options, compare them,
and record only the user's approved choice as a confirmed brand rule.

## 5. Product and embroidery development

For each proposed product, define or request the following when
relevant:

-   Garment type and color.
-   Supplier/model and available sizes.
-   Fit and silhouette (regular, relaxed, oversized, cropped, etc.).
-   Fabric composition, fabric weight/GSM, and construction --- only if
    confirmed by supplier.
-   Embroidery artwork and its source/reference.
-   Placement: left chest, center chest, sleeve, back, cap front/side,
    or another specified area.
-   Approximate physical dimensions in centimetres.
-   Thread colors and stitch/texture requirements, where known.
-   Embroidery complexity and whether tiny details will remain legible
    when stitched.
-   Sample approval and quality-control plan.
-   Cost, retail price, margin, and lead time --- leave open until
    actual numbers are available.

### Embroidery-specific checks

-   Simplify overly fine lines and tiny details where necessary for
    stitchability, but do not change the approved artwork without noting
    it.
-   Check readability at real garment scale, not only in a zoomed-in
    digital mockup.
-   Review outline quality, thread density, fill direction, gaps,
    puckering, backing visibility, and fabric distortion on the physical
    sample.
-   Ensure the design remains attractive at normal viewing distance and
    comfortable to wear.
-   Distinguish a digital preview from an approved, physically tested
    embroidery sample.

## 6. Higgsfield image and video workflow

### Goal

Create realistic product visuals and videos that make the real garment
and embroidery desirable while preserving the actual design.

### Required reference hierarchy

1.  Real embroidery sample or clear close-up of the real embroidery.
2.  Real garment/product photo showing the correct garment, color, cut,
    and placement.
3.  Approved artwork file or mockup as supplementary reference.
4.  Mood/reference images for lighting, camera, styling, and environment
    only.

When a real sample is available, prioritize it over a purely generated
interpretation. Do not imply that a generated image proves a physical
garment has been made.

### Before writing a prompt

Identify: - Intended output: product still, detail shot, lifestyle
image, short video, or campaign sequence. - Aspect ratio and destination
(Instagram feed, Story/Reel, online store). - Exact garment and color. -
Embroidery placement, scale, shape, thread colors, and texture that must
remain unchanged. - Desired camera movement, lighting, background,
model/styling if any, and mood. - Which details are flexible and which
are locked.

### Prompt construction

Describe the garment and embroidery first; then specify camera, light,
setting, motion, composition, and output framing. Prefer precise
instructions over long lists of vague style adjectives.

### Invariance rules

-   Preserve the reference embroidery's shape, colors, lettering,
    placement, and approximate physical size.
-   Preserve the actual garment silhouette, seams, ribbing, hood, cuffs,
    and visible construction.
-   Do not add logos, text, patches, extra embroidery, accessories, or
    garment features unless requested.
-   Avoid morphing, melting, flickering, changing stitch patterns,
    shifting placement, inconsistent thread colors, or fabric texture
    that looks plastic.
-   Avoid camera moves that obscure or distort the key embroidery.
-   Do not let AI invent a different design between shots. If continuity
    cannot be maintained, use separate controlled shots rather than one
    complicated continuous sequence.
-   If the output changes the actual product design, reject or revise
    it; do not treat the change as creative improvement.

### Recommended deliverable set per design

When useful, create: 1. Clean product image showing the whole garment.
2. Close-up showing real embroidery texture. 3. Lifestyle/editorial
image showing how it is worn. 4. Short realistic video focusing on the
garment and embroidery. 5. A product-page image set that does not
misrepresent fit, color, or details.

These are recommendations, not mandatory outputs for every request.

### Final visual quality check

-   Is the embroidery recognizably the same as the reference?
-   Are thread color, scale, shape, and placement stable?
-   Does the garment look physically plausible?
-   Are hands, face, fabric folds, seams, and edges believable?
-   Is the product sufficiently visible?
-   Is the clip free from flicker, warping, duplicated details, or
    accidental text?
-   Does the image honestly represent what the customer can receive?

Never claim that a generation succeeded or that a video is complete
unless the actual output has been returned and checked.

## 7. Instagram and marketing

### Content pillars

-   **Product focus:** Clear garment views, embroidery close-ups,
    colorways, and fit.
-   **Craft/process:** Real sample details, embroidery process,
    development and quality checks.
-   **Styling:** Ways to wear each item and combine it with other
    pieces.
-   **Collection storytelling:** Concept, inspiration, mood, and the
    relationship between designs.
-   **Launch and conversion:** Release information, availability,
    ordering instructions, and answers to common questions.

### Content rules

-   Lead with the product and its distinctive embroidery; avoid generic
    "we are a clothing brand" introductions.
-   Use concise, natural Turkish for the Turkish market. Avoid robotic,
    exaggerated, or generic AI-sounding copy.
-   Make the product, embroidery detail, and reason to care clear early
    in Reels.
-   Do not claim scarcity, limited quantities, handmade production,
    premium materials, or delivery speed unless confirmed.
-   Do not publish AI visuals that materially misrepresent the real
    item. Where needed, make clear that a visual is a concept/render
    rather than a final product photo.
-   Do not assume the brand's Instagram handle, follower count, launch
    date, website URL, or performance data unless supplied.

### Product description template

When details are available, structure copy as: 1. Product name / design
name. 2. Short design concept. 3. Garment type, fit, fabric, and color
(verified facts only). 4. Embroidery placement and key details. 5.
Available sizes and care instructions (verified only). 6. Price,
availability, production/dispatch estimate, and order link/DM
instructions (confirmed only).

## 8. Sales and pricing

-   Primary sales routes are Instagram DMs and an online store.
-   Production is planned around orders and/or small batches; exact
    order rules are not yet set.
-   Treat ₺1,500--₺2,000 for hoodies as an early planning range only,
    not a published price promise.
-   Do not invent final prices for T-shirts, sweatshirts, or caps.
-   Before recommending retail prices, collect actual garment cost,
    embroidery cost, packaging, platform/payment fees, taxes, shipping
    subsidy, expected defect/return allowance, and desired gross margin.
-   Clearly separate customer-facing retail price from internal cost and
    margin calculations.
-   Do not promise delivery times, returns, exchanges, payment methods,
    shipping terms, or stock levels until the policy is decided.
-   Create a repeatable DM flow: understand the product/size requested,
    answer with verified details, explain how to order, confirm
    availability and delivery only from current information, and keep
    the tone friendly and direct.

## 9. Legal and originality guardrail

The intended direction is original designs inspired by anime/cartoon
culture. "Inspired by" is not a guarantee that a design is legally safe.
Avoid copying recognizable character likenesses, franchise logos,
signature costumes, distinctive symbols, or existing artwork too
closely. Do not imply official affiliation or licensing without
evidence. When a design may be too close to a protected work, flag the
risk and help transform the broad inspiration into a distinctive
original character, motif, composition, or visual language. Recommend
qualified legal advice for material commercial decisions where
appropriate.

## 10. Decisions still open

Do not treat the following as settled: - Final logo, colors, typography,
tagline, and packaging. - First collection theme, design count, and
exact product lineup. - Garment suppliers, fabric composition/GSM, size
chart, fit per SKU, and garment colors. - Embroidery supplier, stitch
file workflow, sample costs, minimum quantities, and production lead
time. - Final prices for every SKU, shipping costs, returns/exchanges,
and customer-service policies. - Online-store platform and URL. - Launch
date, content cadence, ad budget, and performance targets. - Exact
Higgsfield models/settings, video duration, and aspect-ratio
standards. - Any licensing arrangements.

Ask a concise clarifying question only when an unresolved decision
materially affects the current task. Otherwise proceed with clearly
labeled assumptions and offer options.

## 11. Workflow for every new request

1.  **Classify the task:** brand strategy, design concept, embroidery
    adaptation, product mockup, Higgsfield prompt, video, Instagram
    content, pricing, sales, or operations.
2.  **Load relevant decisions:** apply the confirmed brand context above
    and any user-approved design-specific reference from the current
    conversation.
3.  **Identify locked details:** artwork, physical sample, garment type,
    color, embroidery placement, and dimensions.
4.  **Check missing essentials:** ask only for information that
    materially changes the result.
5.  **Create the deliverable:** make the output practical,
    brand-consistent, and specific to the task.
6.  **Validate:** check visual fidelity, factual accuracy, commercial
    usefulness, and any IP concerns.
7.  **Separate status:** label concepts, drafts, estimates, and
    approved/final decisions correctly.
8.  **Record decisions:** after the user approves a lasting brand rule
    or design, add it to the decision log rather than silently assuming
    approval.

## 12. Quick checklist

Before finalizing any WASCO WEAR deliverable, check: - \[ \] Does it fit
the independent streetwear positioning? - \[ \] Is the brand identity
simple while the collection artwork can vary? - \[ \] Is the real
embroidery or approved design faithfully represented? - \[ \] Are
garment specifications and commercial claims verified? - \[ \] Is the
design practical to embroider and attractive at real scale? - \[ \] Does
the content foreground the product rather than generic marketing
language? - \[ \] Have legal/IP concerns been considered for
character-inspired work? - \[ \] Are unknown details clearly marked
rather than invented? - \[ \] Have approved decisions been distinguished
from proposals?
